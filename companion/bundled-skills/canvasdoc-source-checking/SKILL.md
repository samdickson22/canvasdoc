# Check sources

Read the relevant source before citing it. Prefer assigned materials for course-specific claims and primary sources for additional research. Search snippets, titles, and another agent's summary do not establish what a source says.

Verify quotations against the original text and confirm page or section references. Distinguish printed page numbers from PDF viewer positions when they differ. Preserve exact quotations during prose editing. Cite the source that supports the specific claim; a real citation can still be irrelevant.

Separate source statements, your explanation, and uncertain inference. If sources disagree, explain the disagreement rather than silently choosing one. If material is inaccessible, identify the gap and continue with what can be supported. Never invent a quotation, citation, page number, or claim to have read missing material.

Keep the check proportional to the task. An explanation of a supplied paragraph does not require an exhaustive literature search. For current deadlines or assignment requirements, consult Canvas and identify stale observations. Return usable source links or workspace paths alongside the claims they support.
